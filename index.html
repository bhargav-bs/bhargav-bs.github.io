A little more context

My path into this work began in the humanities, then moved toward empirical social policy.

I studied literature, linguistics, and philosophy before moving into field-based and data-based research. That shift shaped how I work now: I care about methods, but I also care about language, framing, and whether a piece of research can actually travel beyond a specialist audience.

What I can be useful for

I work best with people who care about public questions and want thoughtful, careful, well-communicated research rather than polished noise. That includes NGOs, independent researchers, media teams, and organisations working in welfare, education, labour, nutrition, and inequality.

What Drives Me

Policy should be shaped by the people it affects.

I want to help build systems that are responsive, fair, and accountable to the communities they serve. For me, good policy research is not only about statistical precision. It is also about understanding how people encounter schools, welfare offices, public services, and institutions in daily life.

My goal is to connect data to stories so that policy is not only well designed, but also legible, actionable, and effective in the communities where it matters most.

Equity and inclusion

Everyone should have meaningful access to rights, opportunities, and public systems.

Participatory governance

Better policy emerges when lived experience and institutional knowledge are taken seriously together.

Evidence-based action

Quantitative analysis matters, but so do narratives, history, and on-ground realities.

Bridging academia and action

Research should travel beyond papers into practice, communication, and public problem solving.

Continuous learning

Serious work stays open to revision, criticism, and better methods as contexts change.

Research

Three strands that have stayed with me across projects.

These are not strict categories so much as recurring concerns in the work: how welfare is administered, how inequality is measured and narrated, and how institutions are actually encountered on the ground.

Current work at Azim Premji University

Welfare regimes, mortality, nutrition, and public systems

At CSIE, I work on welfare and development economics using demographic methods, large-scale public datasets, and policy analysis. Current projects include a paper on cross-state excess mortality during the COVID-19 pandemic, research on adult height and nutrition trends using DHS data, and editorial and data work for a forthcoming Handbook on Welfare in India.

Strand one

Welfare, demography, and measurement

I am interested in how public datasets can be used to understand mortality, nutrition, fertility, welfare systems, and long-run social change across Indian states.

Strand two

Education and rights-based policy implementation

My work on the Right to Education focused on what it takes for a formal entitlement to become a lived, usable right for families navigating institutions in unequal settings.

Strand three

Urban informality, service delivery, and institutional friction

Across projects on digital infrastructure, cooperative models, and livelihood vulnerability, I keep returning to the question of how people experience the state when access depends on unstable systems, mediation, and administrative complexity.
