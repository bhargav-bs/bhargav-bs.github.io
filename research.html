Research

Questions about welfare, inequality, administration, and public systems.

This page focuses on the research side of the profile: current work, recurring strands, and the kind of data work I want to foreground when the site is used as a professional reference.

Research

Three strands that have stayed with me across projects.

These are not strict categories so much as recurring concerns in the work: how welfare is administered, how inequality is measured and narrated, and how institutions are actually encountered on the ground.

Current work at Azim Premji University

Welfare regimes, mortality, nutrition, and public systems

At CSIE, I work on welfare and development economics using demographic methods, large-scale public datasets, and policy analysis. Current projects include a paper on cross-state excess mortality during the COVID-19 pandemic, research on adult height and nutrition trends using DHS data, and editorial and data work for a forthcoming Handbook on Welfare in India.

Strand one

Welfare, demography, and measurement

I am interested in how public datasets can be used to understand mortality, nutrition, fertility, welfare systems, and long-run social change across Indian states.

Strand two

Education and rights-based policy implementation

My work on the Right to Education focused on what it takes for a formal entitlement to become a lived, usable right for families navigating institutions in unequal settings.

Strand three

Urban informality, service delivery, and institutional friction

Across projects on digital infrastructure, cooperative models, and livelihood vulnerability, I keep returning to the question of how people experience the state when access depends on unstable systems, mediation, and administrative complexity.
