About

Background, values, and the shape of the work.

I came into policy research through literature, linguistics, and philosophy, and that path still shapes how I think about evidence, writing, and public argument.

A little more context

My path into this work began in the humanities, then moved toward empirical social policy.

I studied literature, linguistics, and philosophy before moving into field-based and data-based research. That shift shaped how I work now: I care about methods, but I also care about language, framing, and whether a piece of research can actually travel beyond a specialist audience.

What I can be useful for

I work best with teams that care about public questions and clear thinking.

I work best with people who care about public questions and want thoughtful, careful, well-communicated research rather than polished noise. That includes NGOs, independent researchers, media teams, and organisations working in welfare, education, labour, nutrition, and inequality.

Collaborations

I take on research and communication work for teams working on public issues.

If you need someone who can move between messy data, policy context, field realities, and public-facing writing, this is the kind of work I can help with.

Some collaborations are about research design. Some are about cleaning and understanding a difficult dataset. Some are about translating complex findings into a brief, an article, a visual, or a story that can travel. I like work that has to move across those boundaries instead of staying inside one of them.

What Drives Me

Policy should be shaped by the people it affects.

I want to help build systems that are responsive, fair, and accountable to the communities they serve. For me, good policy research is not only about statistical precision. It is also about understanding how people encounter schools, welfare offices, public services, and institutions in daily life.

My goal is to connect data to stories so that policy is not only well designed, but also legible, actionable, and effective in the communities where it matters most.

Equity and inclusion

Everyone should have meaningful access to rights, opportunities, and public systems.

Participatory governance

Better policy emerges when lived experience and institutional knowledge are taken seriously together.

Evidence-based action

Quantitative analysis matters, but so do narratives, history, and on-ground realities. Research should travel beyond papers into practice, communication, and public problem solving.

Continuous learning

Serious work stays open to revision, criticism, and better methods as contexts change.
